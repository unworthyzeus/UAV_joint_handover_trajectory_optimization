\subsection{Illustrative Long Route}
Figures~\ref{fig:example}--\ref{fig:diagnostics} show route
\texttt{53013\_0023}, the longest Euclidean start to goal distance among all
200 declared longer test routes (1,784.7 m). Ties use scenario ID; learned
seed 2101 is retained. Selection uses geometry only. Dense replay of the three
original 200 route batches matched all 600 records exactly; this illustration
adds no independent observations or training.

All three selected flights succeed: original reward takes 85 s and 18 handovers,
full takes 78 s and one handover, and Goal radio takes 77 s and two handovers.
Mean delay proxies are respectively 0.78, 2.79 and 0.87 s. Full's queue
approaches capacity while RSS stays above the minimum. Paths largely overlap
under the shared navigation aid, despite different service behavior. Full's
accumulated radio cost is higher than original on this route, unlike the
average contrast. Aggregate results determine the conclusions.

\section{Interpretation and Limitations}
The contrast isolates the reward package conditional on V2. It does not identify
individual terms or behavior without the navigation prior and prospective
filter. These components may compensate for reward deficiencies. Removing them
would require a separate experiment and fresh evaluation.

Original speed limits, motion, discrete action parameterization, continued
training after arrival and unknown implementation details differ. Reconstructing
the reward on V2 cannot identify the cause of the thesis's wandering
\cite[p.~21, Sec.~8]{thesis}. The dataset is the same as the thesis's;
sentinel meaning remains unverified. One map, static load, ideal service and uncalibrated energy limit
inference. No physical continuity, packet latency, collision safety or deployment
claim follows. A successful arrival can still retain queued data. The initial
comparison uses seeds 53012/53013; the V2.1 followup uses fresh 54012/54013
routes; the supervisor study uses 55012/55013 and the signal path comparison
uses 56012/56013. These test samples are reported
separately. The supervisor has additional map queries and computation; its
performance cannot be attributed to PPO training or reward replacement.

\section{Conclusion}
In the initial V1.5/full V2 comparison, reward replacement does not establish
improved mission completion. Full V2 reduces handovers and flight time but
increases delay and consumed energy. The separate V2.1 service reward improves
delay while missing its completion first gate. Neither result justifies a
reward only diagnosis of the original thesis's behavior.

The controller improvement is conditional on the shared simulator and additional
lookahead computation. Further claims require independent maps, traffic and
controller ablations rather than tuning on these test routes.
